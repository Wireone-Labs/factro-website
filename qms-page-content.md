## The QMS difference

### Less evidence hunting. More time for the quality decision.

- Connected test evidence
- Verified corrective action
- Controlled change

### 01 — One testing history

#### From the incoming sample to the finished batch.

Keep receipt sampling, in-process checks and finished-product testing in one quality system. Results retain their specifications, instrument context and certificate of analysis.

- Cover raw-material, in-process and finished-product tests.
- Keep instruments, calibration and reference standards in view.
- Use OOS and OOT trends to support disposition decisions.

### 02 — Correct the cause

#### A CAPA isn’t closed because someone says it is.

Carry the quality event through investigation, corrective work and an effectiveness check. Keep approvals and evidence attached so closure means the fix has been verified.

- Open deviations, change controls and CAPA from quality events.
- Keep every action with an owner, deadline and conversation.
- Require a passed effectiveness check before CAPA closes.

### 03 — Make change stick

#### The SOP changed. The training should too.

Move documents through draft, review and signed approval with one effective version. When an SOP becomes effective or is revised, training follows the document—not a coordinator’s reminder list.

- Retain controlled revisions and signed approvals.
- Assign training when an SOP takes effect or changes.
- See role-to-SOP competency gaps for each person.

## Inside QMS

### From first sample to final sign-off.

The lab, the investigation, the procedure and the people—one quality system, with evidence at every step.

### 01 — Laboratory & quality control

**Samples, specifications and the evidence behind a result.**

- Manage receipt sampling, IPQC and finished-product testing.
- Retain results against specifications, COAs and OOS/OOT context.
- Manage lab instruments, calibration, upkeep and reference standards.

### 02 — Deviations, CAPA & change control

**A quality event becomes accountable corrective work.**

- Raise quality events automatically or manually with their source context.
- Route corrective records through their configured approvals.
- Require effectiveness to pass before CAPA closure and use trends to support decisions.

### 03 — Controlled documents

**One effective version. An intact history.**

- Move SOPs and safety instructions through draft, review and signed approval.
- Retain version history instead of replacing the previous record.
- Keep document changes connected to required training.

### 04 — Training & competency

**Know who is ready for the work.**

- Assign training when SOPs become effective or are revised.
- Map roles to the SOPs they require.
- Make competency gaps visible per person in the built-in learning system.

### 05 — Instrument qualification

**Keep the evidence with the asset.**

- Hold validation packs alongside laboratory instruments.
- Retain calibration and upkeep records in the quality system.
- Review instrument standing with the testing it supports.

### 06 — Material & batch release

**A quality decision, not a status someone edits.**

- Release incoming material from quarantine after testing passes.
- Require independent QA approval at the final batch-release gate.
- Block batch release while a critical or major deviation remains open.

## Worth knowing

### A closer look at QMS.

### What prevents a CAPA from closing too early?

The effectiveness check must pass and the configured approvals must be completed. Action completion alone does not close the corrective record.

### Does an SOP revision trigger training?

Yes. The built-in learning system assigns training when an SOP becomes effective or is revised. The competency matrix shows the requirements and gaps per person.

### Who makes the release decision?

An independent, authorised QA reviewer. Passing tests does not replace that approval, and an open critical or major deviation blocks batch release.
