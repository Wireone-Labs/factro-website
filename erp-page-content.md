## How it runs

**The business behind every batch**

### Buy with confidence. Move with certainty.

From qualified suppliers to collected payments, keep the commercial and material sides of your plant in step.

### 01 — Buy right

**Purchase & vendor qualification**

#### A better supply chain starts before the PO.

Know who you’re buying from, what they’re approved to supply, and whether they deliver on their commitments.

1. **Qualify the source**  
   Check licences and material-wise vendor approvals before buying.
2. **Connect the commitment**  
   Keep the purchase request, order and vendor bill in one chain.
3. **Measure what arrives**  
   Follow receipt-based OTIF, quality performance and material price trends.

Stores receives an approved order, not a trail of emails.

[Explore purchase](http://localhost:3000/erp#purchase)

### 02 — Receive with control

**GRN, sampling & quality status**

#### On the shelf isn’t the same as ready to use.

Start sampling at receipt. Keep every lot’s identity, location and quality standing together, before anything reaches production.

1. **Identify the receipt**  
   Create the stock control and analytical references against the incoming material.
2. **Sample against the master**  
   Use material-specific sampling tests and approved specifications.
3. **Respect the quality decision**  
   Keep unapproved material out of use, with its status visible to stores.

The quality status travels with the lot, not in a separate register.

[Explore inventory](http://localhost:3000/erp#inventory)

### 03 — Keep stock moving

**Warehouse & stock ledger**

#### Know what you have. And where it can go.

Give every receipt, transfer and issue a place in the stock ledger. Act on retest and expiry reminders before they become production delays.

1. **Locate every lot**  
   Organise stores by warehouse, zone and location, with quality status in view.
2. **Control every movement**  
   Keep relocation, stock audits and code-to-code transfers accountable.
3. **Stay ahead of shortages**  
   Use reorder points, lead times and retest reminders to plan the next move.

The next team sees the material’s location and standing together.

[Explore warehouse](http://localhost:3000/erp#storage)

### 04 — Close the order

**Sales, invoicing & collection**

#### Keep the promise. Keep the whole history.

Connect the customer’s enquiry to the quotation, order, invoice and payment. No separate spreadsheet to reconstruct the commercial story.

1. **Know the customer**  
   Keep licences, brand books and approved pricing with the commercial relationship.
2. **Invoice from the source**  
   Carry the order into invoicing. Enable e-invoices and e-way bills on request.
3. **Complete the collection**  
   Allocate payments and credit notes to the invoice they belong to.

From the first enquiry to the final receipt, the context stays connected.

[Explore sales](http://localhost:3000/erp#sales)

## Inside ERP

### From purchase request to paid invoice.

Sales, purchasing, stores and material masters—built around the controls your plant needs.

### 01 — Sales

**One chain from enquiry to collection.**

- Enquiries, quotations, sales orders and invoices share the commercial history.
- Customer licence checks and approved pricing govern the transaction.
- Payments and credit notes are recorded against their invoice.

### 02 — Purchase

**Know who you can buy from—and how they perform.**

- Qualify vendors per material through the approved vendor list.
- Check vendor licence validity before purchasing.
- Follow requests, orders, bills and debit notes; compare receipt-based OTIF and material price trends.

### 03 — Inventory

**Every receipt, transfer and issue accounted for.**

- Generate stock control and analytical reference numbers at receipt; quarantine the lot and raise sampling.
- Keep disposal, stock audits, relocation and code-to-code transfers controlled.
- Use reorder points, expiry dates and retest reminders to act ahead of shortages.

### 04 — Warehouse

**Model your actual stores, not just a stock total.**

- Organise stock by warehouse, zone and location.
- Keep material identity and quality standing attached to movement.
- Maintain segregation between drug and food dispensing streams.

### 05 — Invoices & payments

**The financial record follows the transaction.**

- Connect customer invoices, payments and credit notes.
- Retain vendor bills and debit notes in the purchase chain.
- Generate statutory invoice and transport documents from the sales workflow when enabled.

e-Invoice and e-way bill generation are available on request and enabled during onboarding.

### 06 — Material masters

**Define it once. Use the approved version.**

- Sampling test masters define how each material is tested, with results against specifications.
- Retain assay per lot and adjust dispensing for that lot’s assay.
- Control changes to materials, vendors and specifications; maintain product pricing, brand and packaging masters.

## Before you start

### Your plant. Your starting point.

Start with the operation you have. Agree the controls and connections you need.

### Does ERP work as a standalone product?

Yes. ERP runs the commercial and material side of the plant on its own, with its own configured controls and workflows.

### Are the control points configurable?

Yes. Configure the relevant controls and approval requirements around your SOPs. When a control is enabled, an action that fails it is refused. Configuration changes are recorded in the audit trail.

### Are e-invoices and e-way bills enabled by default?

No. These capabilities are available on request and can be enabled during onboarding.
