## The MES difference

### Make the batch. Not a second job out of its paperwork.

- The approved process
- Gates that hold
- A record built live

### 01 — Start from the right master

#### The recipe is approved. The batch stays on it.

Turn the master production and control record into a work order pinned to its exact version. Manufacturing, packaging and test requirements become defined stages—not instructions someone has to piece together.

- Design stages with named inputs and outputs.
- Keep MBR, MPR, IPQC and finished-product test requirements together.
- Generate batch documents in your own approved format.

### 02 — Enforce the next step

#### No clearance. No start. No passed check. No next stage.

The process controls are part of execution. Signed line clearance gates the start; in-process quality checks gate the next stage. The system holds the work instead of asking someone to remember a rule.

- Require signed line clearance before the batch starts.
- Keep the next stage held until its IPQC checks pass.
- Capture quantities, times and yields against the plan.

### 03 — Run a ready floor

#### A machine on the plan must be fit for the batch.

Register equipment against the floor it serves. Keep calibration, cleaning, maintenance and breakdown history with the asset, so machine readiness is a condition of use—not a separate spreadsheet.

- Prevent selection when calibration has expired.
- Keep preventive maintenance, cleaning and breakdowns visible.
- Calculate OEE from runtime, downtime and output.

## Inside MES

### Every stage. Every check. Every record.

Planning, execution and equipment readiness stay with the batch. Explore what is built in and what can be connected.

### 01 — Planning & electronic batch records

**From the master to the work actually done.**

- Build formulations and MPCR stages with manufacturing, packaging and testing requirements.
- Create work orders pinned to the approved master version.
- Record planned versus actual quantities, time and yield, and retain each batch’s disposition.

### 02 — Shop floor & equipment

**Readiness, execution and upkeep in context.**

- Register machines against work centres and shop floors.
- Gate use on calibration and gate batch start on signed line clearance.
- Keep cleaning, preventive maintenance, breakdowns and equipment qualification documents together; calculate OEE from operation.

### 03 — Plant data

**Bring machine readings into the batch context.**

- Scope PLC, SCADA, environmental monitoring and IoT data around your plant.
- Use a time-series data store for connected readings.
- Agree the equipment interfaces and data requirements during integration scoping.

Machine and sensor integrations are delivered on request; they are not assumed to be connected out of the box.

### 04 — Integrations

**Connect the systems your plant already runs.**

- Scope plant-floor interfaces around the machines and protocols at your site.
- Discuss SAP or Oracle integration where existing enterprise systems need to remain.
- Agree the records, data flow and responsibilities for each connection before rollout.

Integrations are scoped and delivered per customer.

## Worth knowing

### A closer look at MES.

### What happens if the master changes during a batch?

A work order is pinned to the exact master record version it was created against. Its execution history retains that version rather than silently switching to a newer recipe.

### Can a batch skip a required check?

With the relevant controls enabled, signed line clearance is required before the start and IPQC checks must pass before the next stage. Expired calibration also blocks equipment selection.

### Do our machines need to be connected first?

No. Machine integrations are separately scoped on request. The process, checks and electronic batch record are the core MES workflow.
